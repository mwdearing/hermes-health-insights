# Warning signs on GLP-1 type medicines

Wearable data cannot detect most serious problems; symptoms come first. If the user describes any of these, say so calmly and plainly, do not wait for the next report, and do not try to diagnose.

## Seek urgent or emergency care
- Severe or persistent stomach pain, especially spreading to the back, with or without vomiting (possible pancreatitis).
- Severe pain in the upper right belly with fever, chills, yellow skin or eyes, or persistent vomiting (possible gallbladder problem).
- Vomiting or diarrhea that will not stop, being unable to keep fluids down, very little urine, fainting or severe dizziness (dehydration and kidney strain).
- Swelling of the face, lips or throat, trouble breathing or swallowing, widespread rash, or a fast heartbeat with faintness (severe allergic reaction).
- Shaking, sweating, confusion or blurred vision that does not clear (low blood sugar, especially with insulin or sulfonylureas), or any loss of consciousness.
- Sudden loss of vision or a sudden change in vision.
- Thoughts of suicide or a mood crisis: call or text 988 in the US, or the local emergency number.
- Chest pain, a racing or pounding heartbeat at rest that persists, or fainting.

## Bring to a clinician promptly (not an emergency)
- A lump or swelling in the neck, hoarseness, or trouble swallowing or breathing that persists (the labels carry a boxed warning about thyroid C-cell tumours seen in rodents).
- A resting heart rate that stays well above the person's usual.
- Vision changes in someone with diabetes.
- A planned or possible pregnancy, or breastfeeding. Some products also reduce the effect of oral contraceptives; the prescriber or pharmacist should advise.
- Any operation or procedure under sedation: tell the team about the medicine (it delays stomach emptying) so they can plan. Do not advise about fasting or pausing; the team decides.
- Fast or large weight loss with low food intake, new weakness or lasting tiredness, or low mood.

## How to respond
1. Acknowledge, state that this is informational and not medical advice, and say which list the symptom falls under.
2. For the urgent list: tell them to seek urgent or emergency care now and not to wait for a scheduled check. For the second list: suggest contacting their clinician promptly and offer to summarise relevant data (dates, trends) they can bring.
3. Never say the medicine is or is not the cause, and never advise changing, skipping, delaying, timing or stopping any medication or dose; that is the prescriber's decision. If the user asks, say so and offer to help them prepare questions.
4. Investigational agents have no label; class cautions apply and evidence is limited. Say that honestly.
5. Keep health details out of public places.
